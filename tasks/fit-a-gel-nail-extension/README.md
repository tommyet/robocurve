# Fit and cure a gel nail extension

Fit a prepared clear full-cover extension to a curved practice nail. Seat it against the curved nail, flash-cure it under its matching lamp, then release the hand and complete the cure. The practice insert replaces a natural nail; no person is involved.

The intended physical kit uses Kiara Sky clear Gelly Tips, its Builder gel and Beyond Pro Flash Cure lamp. The manufacturer's guide specifies progressive seating, holding during an initial 10-second cure, then 30 seconds of further exposure. The compact lamp has a 45/60-second timer and a published 66 × 58 × 47 mm body. The simulation uses a 60-second button cycle and at least 40 seconds of exposed gel contact. These timings apply to this compatible system, not arbitrary gels or lamps.

The prototype uses a full-size 28 mm extension, 10–14 mm wide, on a 14 mm curved practice insert. Thin convex sections retain the underside curvature. Thirty-five fixed-thickness 0.24 mm contact patches represent the prepared gel layer on the curved insert. The insert uses tangent facets matching its specified curvature. Wetting allows a 0.15 mm separation margin, smaller than the 0.24 mm gel layer, to approximate a gel bridge across unresolved facet gaps; it is not a measured capillary law. Native collision geometry still prevents interpenetration. This reduced model omits gel flow and compression; contact at the mating surfaces wets a patch, and exposure enables finite native bonds. Once a flash-cured bond remains intact, its wetted interface continues curing under light even during small elastic deflection; a broken bond does not. Gel spill and cured-bond failure remain possible; cracking the rigid practice insert or tip is not simulated. A timer alone cannot align or seat the extension. No task-object pose is assigned during a trial. A contact-gated compliant native grip approximates unresolved friction at the distal pinch, limited to 12 N and 0.030 Nm, with an 8 mm slip limit. This joint connects to the native gripper and transmits reaction forces at each physics step.

This is a reduced material model. It does not simulate chemical conversion, fluid flow or individual air bubbles; unseated regions remain unbonded. The 1 N post-release central pull tests the native bonds. The force acts at the tip's centre of mass, matching the central pull tab; it is not a free-edge peel test. The force is applied through Isaac’s native permanent wrench composer, not a live USD force attribute. An unbonded control verifies that it reaches the solver. Contact stiffness, bond strength and transfer rates are engineering approximations, not measured product data. The practice insert and gel-loading protocol below make the physical reset repeatable; they are proposed test conditions, not a measured calibration of this gel.

A 20 mm spacer beneath the lamp provides wrist clearance. The practice finger is clamped above the lamp floor, with its nail origin at (340, 0, 886.5) mm. The 20 mm finger thickness clears the base; its small proximal clamp is inside the opening and clear of the back wall. The simulated exposure law is not a radiometric calibration at that distance. Physical trials must follow the matched product cure guidance and pass the same 1 N bond test. The 65 mm work riser raises ordinary tools into the robot's workspace; the nail, finger and lamp retain full scale. Both arms use narrow steel finger extensions with silicone faces. The scripted controller reads native object poses and sends ordinary bounded robot joint actions. Dream will receive the same scene, hardware, material model and standard camera/joint observations.

## Physical reset and judging

Use an anatomically shaped silicone practice finger, about70 ×22 ×20mm, clamped at its proximal end. Seat a replaceable ABS nail insert14 ×12mm, curved transversely to a14mm radius, seated in the surrounding silicone. A reusable salon practice hand with replaceable nails is also suitable when one finger is fixed in the photographed pose; [Essential Nails](https://academy.essentialnails.com/nail-trainer/) supplies this type of training hardware. Select and record one insert and extension size for all trials; do not change fit between attempts. The simulator models only the working finger.

Use clear Kiara Sky Gelly Tips of one size, selecting28 ±2mm length and14 ±1mm maximum width. Lightly file the proximal outline to fit the insert before the trial, as ordinary preparation; do not alter its curvature. Remove dust, clean and dry the insert, lightly buff the bonding surfaces, then apply the compatible system's Prep/Primer and leave dry. Apply a thin Builder base coat to the insert and cure for30s before adding the uncured tip coating, following the matched system's preparation sequence. Coat the tip's proximal14 ×12mm underside with0.04 ±0.01mL of the matching Builder gel using a1mL syringe and clean brush. Keep the distal8mm dry for gripping. Start within60s under ordinary room light, with the uncured side down on the support and no existing bond. Use a fresh insert and tip each trial. The robot performs seating, curing and release; preparation is outside the timed task.

The compact matching lamp uses its60s preset after a physical button press. Hold the tip for the initial10s flash cure, release the holding hand, and continue exposure for at least30s; all scored bonded regions need40s total. No live person is used. Put a removable white background behind the clear extension for a final close photograph with a millimetre scale. Outline the14 ×12mm bonding region; visible air pockets or lifted areas count as unbonded. Measure bonded area with a1mm grid. Match the proximal-edge midpoints within1.5mm in the nail plane, and keep the extension within8degrees of the insert orientation. The extension projects beyond the nail at its free edge; their geometric centres are not expected to coincide. Check for gel on the silicone around the insert; any spill or damaged insert/tip scores0.

After both hands have been clear for3s, attach a light removable pull tab to the centre of the extension (14mm from its proximal edge, on the centreline), then apply1N perpendicular to the nail with a spring scale: ramp over0.5s, hold3s, unload over0.5s. Record a close video with a millimetre scale throughout loading; any peak lifting or movement above1mm scores0, even if it returns after unloading. If intact, aligned and retained, bonded area of90% earns1.0,75% earns0.5,50% earns0.25; less earns0. A held tip, uncured gel or an incomplete proof test earns0. No hidden chemical sensor is needed. The35-patch simulation approximates these visible bonded/unbonded areas rather than resolving fluid bubbles.
